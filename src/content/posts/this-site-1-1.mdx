In an endless sea of app updates, it wouldn't be right if I didn't announce version 1.1 of this site within a month of launch. How would the investors know we're doing cool things?

In software engineering we like to manufacture false drama for the marketing team. That usually means a post-release content patch, or a whole set of features we realised were important but didn't release, because of reasons (usually resourcing). The truth is that most of the time we set the criteria for an <abbr title="Minimum viable product">MVP</abbr>, deliver that as the first release or a major one, and then follow it up with at least a .1 release, carrying the features and fixes that didn't make the MVP sprint[^scrum-guide-sprint].

This post celebrates that tradition. I decided on the MVP that launched the site and immediately started a list of things to change. I wasn't alone in it, either: the team behind this website had feedback of its own. I guess it's time to meet the team, because nothing in modern agentic AI workflows is a one-person effort any more.

But first, a classic list of release notes for your reading (or listening) pleasure.

## Release notes: 1.1, already

This release of _Tim writes [about] dev_ includes [many things](/projects/this-site/) that will excite and awe you:

### 📢 Improving your experience (with features no one asked for yet)

- Most pictures can now go full screen (wow, pixels!)
- Tables can now go full screen on mobile (much data, very interesting)
- Generative AI images now carry a burnt-in label (in the spirit of the EU AI Act[^eu-ai-icons])
- The posts page now has a jump list for years and months
- A short version up top, for the <abbr title="Too long; didn’t read">TL;DR</abbr> crowd and anyone who needs a refresher or a hook to read on
- Basic diagrams can now be used in posts (there's one in [Meet the cast](#meet-the-cast))
- Posts and pages narrated in full, by Brian (more on him below)
- The logo is now the identity matrix
- Mathematical symbols drift in the margins, for the wide-screen readers
- Posts were turned into cards (because cards are better for content discoverability!)

### 🐛 Squashing of bugs (they were _features_ once)

- Wide tables were cropped on mobile browsers
- The <abbr title="Really Simple Syndication">RSS</abbr> feed and the sitemap showed as a wall of text instead of something pretty
- Images broke in Edge's Immersive Reader
- Pesky em dashes were swapped out, because apparently they're too generative AI for a site about agentic AI
- The home page was made less boring
- About was made less boring (read all the things)

### 🏆 Things we kept the same, but they seem new so they're in anyway

- No analytics and no third-party anything
- No special scripts for the narration audio, just smart serving of static content[^http-range-requests] and modern browsers

- **Tim (this is me obviously, but it's a version I construct for our flourishing parasocial relationship! Yay!):** A mysterious figure, only ever seen from behind (or blacked out), never in portrait, who spends countless hours developing the content by typing vigorously on a keyboard. A holder of <abbr title="Master of Science"><span lang="la">_Magister Scientiae_</span></abbr> in Artificial Intelligence.
- **Opus (Anthropic's Claude model):** Opus is a jack of all trades, and only _great_ at them (not a _master_, that would be a <span lang="la">_magister_</span> in Latin).
  - Collaborator - acts as a sounding board for post ideas and turns after-work-brain Tim into consumable content (yum!).
  - Developer - happily writes all the code that keeps the site serving content and our parasocial relationship alive.
  - Proofreader - basically a secretary, whose job is to turn my poor grammar, spelling mistakes and occasional awkward prose (because who doesn't write slower than they think?) into readable human thought. Also makes sure I don't sound like I have multiple voices (brand consistency!).
  - Reader - an ~unwilling~ instance of Opus (a sub-agent[^claude-code-subagents], for those familiar with the term) that reviews the page as if it was one of ye. It usually complains about my jokes, and its feedback I ~always~ sometimes ignore.
  - Researcher - as I lack a photographic memory, Opus will go and fact-check anything that's sus[^sus] or that ye might doubt.
- **Ms Claudie Mittens:** a graceful hypothetical cat, once a kitten, who has eaten all the cookies on the site and happily walks across the keyboard as part of her classical music training. She's the team's mascot, named after Claude, and Mittens because it rhymes with Claudie (I don't know how rhyme).
- **Brian, an AI voice:** our multilingual expert neural voice. Brian speaks 92 languages and accents, and Microsoft bills him as a sincere, calm and approachable young man, made for podcasts and chat[^brian-voice-gallery]. But to us here on _Tim writes [about] dev_, he's just Brian, an AI voice. He narrates everything here, and every day is a new day for him, since Brian has no [texturiser](/posts/texturiser/).

## What's next

Like all good services, here's a hopeful list of things that will probably happen at some vague, unspecified time in the future:

- A timeline for the projects page (scrolling)
- A fancy search for posts and projects, all powered by JavaScript (sorry Opus, that'll be another script!)
- Support for more kinds of media in posts, like video
- More AI voices, perhaps a Peking opera narration[^peking-opera] for extra drama? 😱

Just like your favourite TV show from the 1950s (not the Bradys, they're far too modern), we'll go on releasing new versions, adding background characters and setting off on non-canon adventures, all while keeping both our readers and our investors happy!
